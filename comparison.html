Platform decision RegenCare content platform 9 Sep 2026

WordPress and its plugins, or Payload CMS 3.x

Recommendation

Build on Payload CMS 3.x. Do not adopt WordPress.

Not because headless is fashionable. On plugin capability the two stacks are nearly even — so the decision is made by security, clinical governance, and what happens at several hundred articles.

What actually decides it

01
Volume changes which properties matter

Every criticism of WordPress here is survivable at twenty pages. wp_postmeta scans are fast on a small table; Elementor's DOM weight costs one page's load time. At several hundred articles with faceted filtering and a publishing cadence, the same properties become structural — meta queries that cannot be indexed well, and a DOM tax paid on every indexed page. The pivot did not change the platforms. It changed which of their properties matter.

02
Five plugins is the attack surface

WordPress core has an excellent record — six low-risk issues last year. The risk is the plugin supply chain, and our stack would add five independently-maintained codebases with database access inside the admin. For a clinical brand, a defacement is a reputational event, not an outage.

03
Clinical claims need an enforced reviewer

MedicalWebPage.reviewedBy is the credentialing signal Google and AI answer engines use to judge medical trustworthiness. In WordPress it is a field an editor types into and can leave blank. In Payload it is a required relationship to a real clinician record, and access control blocks publishing without it. The same mechanism gives each AI agent a draft-only identity — the restriction is declared once on the collection, so it applies to every route in, not just the button in the interface.

The security numbers

WordPress ecosystem, 2025 — from Patchstack's State of WordPress Security in 2026.

91% of vulnerabilities are in plugins

Out of 11,334 found last year, up 42% year on year. Core accounted for six, all low risk.

5 hrs median time to first exploitation

20% of heavily exploited flaws are hit within six hours of disclosure; 70% within a week.

46% disclosed with no patch available

Nearly half go public while the plugin is still unfixed. Hosting defences block only a fraction.

What we give up by choosing Payload

These are real costs, not rhetorical ones. Budget for them.

WordPress would launch sooner

A theme plus these five plugins is weeks. Components take longer. That is a real cost, not a rhetorical one.

We hand-write the JSON-LD

Rank Math's schema builder is better. We write ours once, generated from typed relationships.

Clinic staff can't add content types

Every schema change becomes a developer task and a deploy.

No readability scoring or 404 monitoring

Replace with Screaming Frog, Ahrefs or Search Console rather than pretending the gap isn't there.

Payload developers are harder to hire

Mitigated by documenting heavily — PROJECT_DETAILS.txt is the start.

The one finding that reverses this

If marketing must be able to create and restructure content types without engineering involvement, then WordPress with ACF Pro and CPT UI is the right answer and this recommendation should be discarded.

That is the only requirement we found that flips the verdict, and it is a business question rather than a technical one. It should be settled before build starts.
