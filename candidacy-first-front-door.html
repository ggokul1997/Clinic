UX Architecture Assessment · Regencare · Kochi · Kozhikode · Chennai

The Candidacy-First Front Door

Your read is right: Regencare has essentially no acute traffic, and the emergency triage machinery in the parent study should be deleted rather than softened. But the architectural discipline survives the deletion — you simply swap the axis. A general hospital sorts visitors by how urgent. An elective regenerative clinic sorts them by how convinced.

Candidacy is Regencare's acuity. The front door's job is not "where do I go" but "is this real, is it real for me, and can I trust the person telling me?" — and the site that answers those three in order wins the ₹1.5 lakh decision.

01 — The axis swap

Delete the four doors. Build three stages and one fast path.

In the parent study, the homepage's central job was an acuity read: a stressed person classifying their own urgency in under five seconds, using layout rather than labels. Regencare has no such user. Nobody arrives at a regenerative medicine clinic's website mid-crisis — with one exception, addressed below.

What replaces it is a conviction read. Your visitor arrives somewhere on a three-stage journey, and the stages have requirements as sharply incompatible as the acuity tiers did:

The exception to your premise: diabetic foot ulcer

You listed Diabetic Foot Ulcer among your verticals, and it is the one offering where delay has a limb-threatening cost. A patient or caregiver searching "foot ulcer not healing" is closer to the acute-mode user in the parent study than to your elective aesthetics patient: comprehension is degraded by fear, and the correct design response is a short, high-contrast, single-instruction path with a stated same-day response commitment — not a candidacy essay. Keep exactly one acute-mode pattern from the parent study, and apply it only here.

Why the sequence matters more than the segments

The parent study's four doors were parallel — a visitor belonged to one and never touched the others. Regencare's three stages are serial: the same person passes through all three, usually across multiple sessions and often across two devices and one family conversation. This has a concrete architectural consequence that no general-hospital pattern covers.

Design for resumption, not for a session. A ₹1.5 lakh elective decision is made over days or weeks. That means: no funnel that assumes single-session completion; a shareable, sendable artefact at every stage (a condition page a daughter can forward to her father, a written candidacy opinion the family can discuss, a cost sheet they can compare); and WhatsApp as the thread that survives across sessions — which is precisely why it functions as your patient portal even though it is not one.

04 — Credibility architecture

The finding that should reorder your roadmap

I read your stem cell therapy page. It is headlined "Stem Cell Therapy – Your Body's Natural Healing Power", states that stem cells "enable your body to heal itself", claims treatment of arthritis, ligament tears and cartilage degeneration alongside "glowing skin and fuller hair", promises "long-lasting results" that address root causes, quotes ₹1,50,000 as a starting price, carries four first-name patient anecdotes, and offers a free consultation. It contains no evidence citations, no candidacy criteria, no risks, no adverse-effect discussion, and no statement of regulatory status.

Set aside marketing judgement for a moment; the structural problem is that this page is the least persuasive asset you own for the visitor who matters most. The person about to spend ₹1.5 lakh on an autologous knee procedure has already read the skeptical coverage of stem cell clinics. To that reader, an unhedged cure page is not evidence of confidence — it is the exact signature of the operators they were warned about. You are being penalised by your own strongest patients for a page written to convert your weakest.

Risk flag — not legal advice, and worth putting in front of counsel

Three regimes bear on this content, and they point the same way. ICMR's National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research (2017) classify any stem cell use in patients other than haematopoietic reconstitution for approved indications as investigational, and include provisions restricting publicity claiming available cures using stem cell products. The Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 remain the operative professional-conduct rules — the NMC's 2023 replacement, which would have explicitly barred patient testimonials, was placed in abeyance in August 2023 and is not in force — and clause 6.1.1 prohibits direct or indirect patient solicitation through self-aggrandising advertising. The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 attaches criminal liability to advertising cures for its scheduled conditions. Your "Trusted by Celebrities" page and your named-patient "Success Stories" are the assets most exposed to the second regime; the stem cell and cultured-osteoblast pages to the first.

I am not a lawyer and this is a flag rather than an opinion. But it is a flag that should change your build order, because the fix is also the best UX move available to you.

4.1 The Evidence Ladder

Your seven "Next-Gen Procedures" are currently presented as one confident tier. They are not one tier. PRP for knee osteoarthritis sits in a large, contested, and — importantly — protocol-dependent literature: the RESTORE trial in JAMA (2021, n=288) found no significant benefit over saline placebo, while a 2025 meta-analysis of roughly 2,000 randomised participants found significant improvement in pain and function at 6 and 12 months, with effect size influenced by platelet concentration, prompting a 2025 Lancet commentary arguing the "no evidence" position is no longer supportable. Autologous cultured osteoblasts and SVF sit somewhere quite different. Presenting both at the same confidence level is what makes the whole set unbelievable.

The fix is a standardised component on every procedure and condition page — an honest tier badge plus four fixed fields. It should be authored by your clinical team, not by a marketing writer, and the tier assignments below are illustrative of the method; your clinicians and counsel must set the actual values.

TierDefinitionWhat the page must say
Established Routine clinical use with a substantial randomised literature, whatever the residual debate about effect size. What the evidence shows, where it is contested, and — for PRP specifically — your preparation protocol and platelet concentration, since that is the variable the meta-analytic signal turns on.
Emerging Promising but thinner evidence; smaller trials, shorter follow-up, or heterogeneous protocols. Explicitly: "the evidence base here is thinner than for PRP." Expected benefit stated as a range, with follow-up duration named.
Investigational Regulatory status in India is investigational — including non-haematopoietic stem cell applications. Regulatory status stated plainly, the governing framework named, no cure or outcome claim, and no price on a page that makes a claim it cannot support.

Counterintuitively, this raises conversion among high-value patients. A clinic that publishes where its own evidence is weak is the only kind of clinic that survives a skeptical reader's scrutiny — and it is the only differentiator in this market that a competitor cannot copy by writing better ad copy.

4.2 Trade testimonials for outcomes

This is the strategic recommendation in this study. The University of Utah move in the parent study was not really "publish reviews" — it was "own your own transparency surface rather than ceding it." That move is available to you in a form that is both more persuasive and less exposed.

Retire the named-patient "Success Stories" and the "Trusted by Celebrities" page. Replace them with a Results & Evidence section publishing, per condition and per procedure:

  • Number of patients treated and number of procedures, by condition, with the date range.
  • Mean change in a named, standard instrument — WOMAC or KOOS for knee, VAS for pain, and a comparable scale for the dermatology work.
  • Follow-up duration, and the proportion lost to follow-up. Stating the second is what makes the first believable.
  • The proportion who went on to require surgery anyway. Publishing this is the single most credible thing on your website.
  • Known adverse events and their rate.
  • A plainly written "Who we don't treat" page — the conditions and patient profiles you decline, and why.

You have 25,000+ procedures and 35,000+ patients over ten years. Almost nobody in Indian regenerative medicine can produce a dataset of that size, and right now you are spending it on three round numbers in a hero strip. Turned into published outcomes, that same dataset becomes the reason a surgery-avoiding patient in Dubai books a video consult with you rather than the clinic down the road.

4.3 Clinician credibility, built properly

With two clinicians and no directory to build, all the effort that a hospital spends on filters goes into two pages. Each needs: NMC or state medical council registration number, full qualifications with awarding institutions, fellowship and specific regenerative-medicine training, years in practice, personal procedure volumes by type, publications and talks, professional memberships, languages spoken, and which centres they practise at on which days. Dr Vineeth's orthopaedic surgical credential is a particularly important signal to the surgery-avoider — a surgeon recommending a non-surgical route carries a weight that a non-surgeon cannot — and it deserves to be made explicit rather than left implicit in "MS Ortho".

06 — Booking architecture

You are not booking a procedure. You are booking an assessment.

This is the load-bearing distinction, and it resolves the tension between "adopt One Medical's frictionlessness" and "this is a ₹1.5 lakh decision". A procedure cannot be self-booked: it needs imaging review, candidacy assessment and informed consent. So the front door's conversion event is an assessment, and there are four assessment products at four different commitment levels.

Say this on the site, plainly: "Procedures are scheduled after your assessment, not before." It removes the sales-funnel smell and it is simply true.

What to fix in the current form, concretely

  1. Add the three fields that determine everything: which centre, which vertical (orthopaedics or dermatology), and who the enquiry is for (myself / someone I care for). The third is not a nicety — the proxy state is a large share of your traffic and every downstream message is wrong without it.
  2. Replace the free-text date. A required YYYY-MM-DD field is a known mobile failure and it is asking the patient to guess your availability. Show real slots, or ask for a preference range.
  3. Fix the submit label. "SUBMITSubmitting form" is a rendering bug, and the button should say what happens: "Request a callback" if a human calls back, "Book" only if it writes to a calendar.
  4. State the response window. "We reply on WhatsApp within 15 minutes, 8am–9pm IST" converts; silence does not.
  5. Add a DPDP-shaped consent notice — purpose, what you collect, how long you keep it, who it is shared with, and how to withdraw. Health information is personal data, and this applies to the WhatsApp channel as much as the web form.
  6. Add file upload for imaging on the report-review path, with a stated size limit and accepted formats, and a WhatsApp fallback for anyone who finds upload hard.
  7. Confirm properly. On submit: what happens next, when, from which number, and the centre's location object — building, entrance, floor, parking, and an "if you are running late" line.
A recommendation with a real trade-off

Charge a modest, refundable consultation fee via UPI rather than offering "free consultation". It reduces enquiry volume — that is the cost, and it is real. What it buys is a materially higher show-rate, a filter against tyre-kickers, and, most importantly, a positioning signal: free consultations are the convention of sales-led aesthetic chains, and a boutique clinic charging for a surgeon's time reads as a clinic rather than a funnel. Given that your credibility problem is the binding constraint, I would take that trade. Keep the WhatsApp enquiry free and instant, so nobody is priced out of asking a question.

08 — The India layer

What replaces the US compliance and channel assumptions

8.1 WhatsApp is the architecture, not an add-on

Your site already links WhatsApp, but it is treated as one contact option among several. In the Indian market it is the primary channel, and the numbers are decisive: 540 million-plus users, dominant across age groups in urban healthcare demographics, 30–40% more after-hours bookings than phone-only, and a 3.2× conversion advantage for a four-minute reply over a four-hour one, with booking rates falling sharply after an hour. Patients also will not install a clinic app, which closes off the route a US system would take.

  • Give WhatsApp utility-bar primacy on every page, with a stated response window.
  • Use WhatsApp Business API with templated flows: enquiry triage, report intake, appointment confirmation, pre-procedure instructions, session-series reminders, and post-procedure follow-up.
  • Route by vertical and centre from the first message, so the patient never repeats themselves — the Redundant Entry principle applies to a chat thread exactly as it applies to a form.
  • Treat the four-minute response target as an operational commitment with staffing behind it. Publishing a promise you cannot keep is worse than publishing none.
  • Bring the same consent discipline to WhatsApp as to the web form. Health data collected in chat is still personal data.

8.2 Language

Malayalam for Kochi and Kozhikode, Tamil for Chennai, English for urban and Gulf NRI traffic. This is a conversion issue before it is an inclusion one: your highest-value segment — the 55-to-75-year-old osteoarthritis patient weighing a knee replacement — is the segment most likely to want Malayalam and least likely to persevere in English. Human-translate the condition pages, the candidacy router and the consent notices; do not machine-translate clinical content. Label the switcher in the target language ("മലയാളം", "தமிழ்"), set lang attributes correctly, and translate the WhatsApp templates and confirmation messages — a Malayalam enquiry answered by an English confirmation has failed at the last step. Arabic is worth considering for the Gulf corridor once the core three are solid.

8.4 Performance and device reality

Mobile-dominant, Android-heavy, on networks that vary widely between a Kochi apartment and a Kozhikode village. Set a homepage weight budget and hold it, target largest-contentful-paint under 2.5 seconds on a mid-tier Android over 4G, and treat imaging upload as the most fragile flow on the site — chunked upload, resumable, with a clear WhatsApp fallback for anyone whose connection or confidence gives out.

Demote the procedure pages to references. This is the structural change and the SEO change at once.

Slower than the rest — it needs clinical work, not web work — but it is the differentiator no competitor can write their way to.
